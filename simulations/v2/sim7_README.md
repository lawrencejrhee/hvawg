# Sim 7: Simulation Coverage of the Final V2 Circuit

This sim checks that every final V2 value is covered by a passing simulation.

## Question

Is every final V2 value in the signal and HV path (hvawg.kicad_sch as of 2026-09-24) covered by a passing simulation? Sims 1 to 5 ran before several values changed (R30 10k to 22k, R29 to 1Mohm, R22 50 to 75ohm in Sim 2), and none of them simulated the whole chain.

## Short answer

- Yes. Every final value in the signal and HV path is simulated and passes, at ±150V and, where the bringup uses them, ±50V (and ±120V for centering).
- Three findings need attention. None of them calls for a value change; finding 1 changed R30's package.
  - Finding 1: R30 = 22k in a tap-to-+HV short. U8 is protected (6.3 to 6.6mA, below its 10mA absolute maximum), but R30 dissipates 0.87 to 0.97W. The 0805 first planned for R30 would overheat in a sustained short (it would fail open, which is safe for U8). R30 is therefore a 2W 2512, TE CRGP2512F22K (22k 1%, 500V, pulse-rated thick film; LCSC C2076055), which takes this fault at about half its rating.
  - Finding 2: changing rails without re-trimming costs more than Sim 5 said. R30's 22k carries U8's input bias current, which lowers TP3 by a fixed amount that does not scale with the rail. That is about 5mV with a typical NE5532 (0.2uA x 22k), and 11 to 18mV with the NE5532 model and at maximum bias. A board trimmed at ±50V and run at ±150V sits at -2.9 to -4.4V (TP5 at midscale), and down to -5.2V with a max-bias NE5532. For the pairing Sim 5 covered (±50V to ±120V), Sim 7 gives -2.0 to -3.1V against Sim 5's -0.5 to -1.5V (ideal U8, no R30), about 1.5V worse. The NE5532 model biases at about 0.46 to 0.5uA, above the datasheet typical of 0.2uA, so typical parts show less. Trim RV1 at the rail you will use.
  - Finding 3: RV2 has a usable window of about 0.2 to 0.5kohm. At its ends (0 or 1kohm) no RV1 setting in 3.3 to 4.3kohm can center the output. The bringup setting of about 0.29kohm is inside the window.
- The output stage is stable with the final values (R22 75ohm, R18, C24/C25). With the IRF730 model's own Crss (about 52pF, nearly flat out to 150 to 280V) the phase margin is 66 to 79° for CL 10 to 100pF at TP6, and 50 to 59° with a 20pF Crss floor. There is no oscillation or limit cycle, including with a 1nF load.
  - Those margins are not a worst case. A real IRF730's Crss falls well below 20pF at high VDS. A separate check with a realistic Crss (about 22, 12 and 7pF) gives about 20 to 47°. The stage is still stable, but a step can overshoot on the Q1 source current (about 30% at the lowest Crss) and ring at 2 to 3MHz for about 2us; TP5 itself shows no overshoot. On the bench, probe TP10 (Q1 source) and TP9 (gate) as well as TP5. That check is not in the repo, and its Crss values were assumed, not read off the datasheet curve.
  - The out and osc groups of Sim 7 did show limit cycles. They are an artifact of the cshunt=1e-12 option inherited from Sim 2 (see "The cshunt artifact" in section 2).

- Crossover is 1.8 to 2.4MHz, consistent with Sim 6 (77° at 10pF).
- Crss floor of 20pF instead of 50pF: phase margin is 59 / 53 / 50° at 22 / 47 / 100pF, with no ringing. This is not a pessimistic bound: with a realistic high-VDS Crss the margin is about 20 to 47° (see the short answer).
- R22 = 50ohm (Sim 2's value) at 100pF: phase margin 61.5°. So R22 = 75ohm is slightly better.
- At ±50V the stage reaches 90.4Vpp at 10pF, with THD 7.5%.
- Q1 gate-source voltage stays at 1.7 to 3.7V. The D4 zener current is at most 42uA, which is just its junction capacitance.

The cshunt artifact:
- The copies of the Sim 2 netlists keep `.options cshunt=1e-12`. LTSpice adds that 1pF to every node, including the internal nodes of the LM8261 macro-model, whose only explicit capacitor is its 8pF Miller cap.
- With it, the U7 loop falls to a phase margin of -11 to +25° at about 1.1MHz.
- The step runs then show a sustained 0.14 to 0.56Vpp limit cycle at TP5 for CL of 33pF and up.
- With the 20pF Crss floor as well, they show a 27 to 30Vpp self-oscillation at 2.2MHz (groups out and osc).
- Removing only that option (group nocs, results above) restores 66 to 79° and removes every limit cycle.
- The full-chain transients and Sim 6 never used cshunt, and they agree with nocs.
- Conclusion: the with-cshunt results double-count parasitics in a vendor-fitted macro-model. They are kept only as a record.
- As a cheap bench check at bringup, look at TP5 at zero input and after a small DAC step with the real cable on, and check for 1 to 3MHz ringing at TP10 and TP9 as well as TP5.

## 3. What is still not simulated, and why it does not matter

- U4B and U8B tie-offs (grounded followers). These op-amp halves are not in any signal path. They only draw quiescent current.
- DAC0808 internals (C8 100nF COMPEN, R6, output compliance and glitches) and U1/U2 (7805, 74HC595). The DAC is modeled as an ideal current sink. This path is unchanged from V1, which produces correct waveforms on the bench, and no V2 value is involved.
- Supply decoupling C1 to C13, C20 to C23, C28 to C31 and the bleeders R24/R25. The sims use stiff sources with 1ohm series resistance, so these parts have no effect on the signal path.
- LED indicators D1 to D3 and R1 to R3. They are off the signal path. R3/D3 load the external floating 12V supply by about 4mA.
- R32/C32 test load (DNP). Its values are chosen at bringup and are not part of the design. R19/R23 must stay DNP (Sim 1).
- R21 parasitics (the flat LTO 50 in HS1): see Sim 6. The output-node copper added by the flat layout is covered in V2-SUMMARY.md section 2.8.
- Thermal behavior (Q1, R21, heatsink): not a circuit simulation; see V2-SUMMARY.md section 2.8.
- Model limits. Q1 and Q3 use datasheet VDMOS approximations; the Crss floor and Vth spread were varied. The LM8261 and NE5532 use their vendor macro-models.

## 4. Method and runs

Templates. These are copies of the Sim 2/3/4 netlists; the originals are untouched.
- `sim7_out_sine.cir`, `sim7_out_step.cir`, `sim7_out_loop.cir`: the output stage.
- `sim7_ls_common.inc` with `sim7_ls_ac.cir`, `sim7_ls_step.cir`, `sim7_ls_sine.cir`: the level shifter.
- `sim7_mon_fault.cir`, `sim7_mon_powerdown.cir`: the monitor.
- `sim7_chain_dc.cir`, `sim7_chain_tran.cir`: the new full-chain netlists.

Loop-gain break: the out_loop netlists break the loop with a voltage injection between the Q1 source (low-Z) and U7 -IN (high-Z). The phase of V(src)/V(fb) where its magnitude is 1 is the phase margin.

Runner: `sim7_run.py` generates each case into `sim7_runs/` and runs every netlist as its own `LTspice -b` process.
- Each process has a 240s hard timeout and a 60s no-CPU watchdog, and only its own process tree is killed.
- Logs are checked for freshness and decoded from UTF-16.
- The DC .op search is retried through documented options only (noopiter, gminsteps=0, srcstepmethod=1), or through an HV homotopy (the .op is solved at ±150V and the rails are then ramped).
- Every case is flagged OP FAILED if the .op is skipped.
- Results go into `sim7_results.json`.

Final data: 196 cases, 196 ok, 0 timeouts, 0 unexpected .meas failures.
- The failures that do appear are fring/t_set WHEN-measures that fail because there is no ringing, or because the edge is already inside the band.
- 38 cases needed a second .op route: 30 used the HV homotopy and 8 were no-nodeset AC runs.
- Cases can time out when LTSpice runs next to another LTSpice batch (the four 150V sine runs did while Sim 6 was running). They pass when rerun.
- The runner also has an osc2 group (in-loop compensation, with cshunt). It was dropped once the cshunt artifact was found and is not in `sim7_results.json`.

Groups in `sim7_results.json`:
- mon: 4
- out: 37 (with cshunt)
- osc: 28 (with cshunt)
- nocs: 50 (without cshunt)
- ls: 17
- chain1/chain2: 54
- ctran: 6
