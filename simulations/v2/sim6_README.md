# Sim 6: R21 Parasitics at ±150V

This sim checks how R21's parasitics affect the V2 output stage at ±150V.

## Question

R21 (the 10kohm pull-up from +HV to the Q1 drain at TP5) is laid flat on the V2 board. One candidate part was a V1-style 25W aluminum-housed wirewound with its housing grounded. Could that part's series inductance, element-to-housing capacitance or self-capacitance slow the output edges or make the stage oscillate?

## Short answer

- No oscillation in any case. 61 cases were run, from L = 0 to 5mH and Ccase = 0 to 100pF, with the housing grounded or floating, Cpar 0 to 3pF, and Cload 10 or 30pF.
- The speed cost comes from the element-to-housing capacitance when the housing is grounded. Rise and fall stay within 10% of ideal up to about 15pF (Cload 10pF) or about 10pF (Cload 30pF).
- Series inductance is harmless up to about 0.5mH. Everything in the 0 to 200uH target range stays within 3.3%.
- Grounding the housing is fine for stability. Whether it is fine for speed depends on the part's real Ccase, which should be measured.

## Setup

- Stage: the same topology as `sim2_classA_v2_irf730.cir` (VAR=3). U7 LM8261 on a floating 12V rail, Rg1 1ohm into the IRF730 approximation, R28 100k and D4 BZT52C12 for gate protection (Cz 45pF), R31 4.7k to TP6, and a 10Mohm scope plus Cload at TP6. The models are copied verbatim from Sim 2.
- Board values, checked against `hvawg.kicad_sch`:
  - R21 10k, R22 75ohm (Sim 2 used 50ohm, so this is a change), R31 4.7k, R28 100k, D4 BZT52C12, Q1 IRF730.
  - R18 1Mohm from gate to -HV is added (it is a board part).
  - Rails are ±150V, each with 1ohm source resistance plus C26/C27 4.7uF (1ohm ESR).
  - The `cshunt=1e-12` option is removed. It adds 1pF from every node to ground, which would have put about 9 to 19pF of "Ccase" into the ladder even in the ideal case.
  - The U7 input is an ideal source referenced to -HV, as in Sim 2.
- Transcription check: `sim6_validate_sim2.cir` rebuilds Sim 2 (VAR=3, Cload 10pF, Cz 45pF) with this generator. It reproduces step 4 of `sim2_classA_v2_irf730.log` exactly: TP5 pp 278.641, TP6 pp 278.220, max 141.695, min -136.946.
- R21 model: 10kohm total, built as 5 T-sections. Each section is 1k + L/10, then a middle node, then 1k + L/10. Ccase/5 runs from each middle node to the housing node, and Cpar runs end to end from +HV to TP5. The housing is either grounded (node 0) or floating (1Gohm to +HV and 1Gohm to GND). When L = 0 the inductors are left out, so there are no 0H parts.
- One combined transient per case (640us, 5ns max step, gear), with 5ns input edges:
  - (a) 0 to 60us: 100kHz sine sized for ±140V DC-equivalent at TP5, measured over 20 to 60us.
  - (b) Large step: -100V to +100V at 90us and back at 140us. Measured: 10-90% rise and fall at TP5 and TP6, overshoot, settling to 1%, and ringing (hysteresis zero-crossings).
  - (c) Zero input: 0V from 190us, with a 200us window (240 to 440us) checked for pp and spectrum. Then a +50mV input step (about -6.7V out) and back.
  - (d) DC at -140V (520us) and +140V (580us), checked for pp and R21 power.
- A separate loop-gain AC run per case: Middlebrook double injection with the loop broken between the U7 output and Rg1, evaluated at TP5 = +140, 0 and -140V.
- Metrics are computed with numpy from the .raw files, which are then deleted. The .meas values in the .log files are a cross-check: pp and power agree to 1e-12. The rise and fall .meas use fixed ±80V levels, so they differ from the numpy 10/90% values by at most 2%.
- The transient and loop netlists include a `.nodeset` initial guess. In the transients it changes the results by at most 1e-4 relative (checked against earlier runs of 57 cases without it). Without it, the loop netlists' DC operating point takes about 100s.
- Each netlist runs as its own LTSpice process, with a 240s timeout and a 60s CPU-idle hang watchdog, and only its own process tree is killed.

Pass criteria:
- Timing: the 10-90% rise and fall times at TP5 and TP6 are within 10% of ideal (in either direction).
- Overshoot: the TP5 large-step overshoot is at most 5 percentage points above ideal.
- Stability: TP5 pp is under 50mV in every flat window, and every edge settles to 1% before the next event.

The power figures match (150 - V)^2/10k (2.27W and 8.44W). They are the same in every case, because the parasitics carry no DC current.

With Cload 30pF the ideal case gives rise / fall of 1.495 / 1.904us at TP5, and a TP6 sine of 245.6Vpp.

### Why the edges behave as they do

The stage is a current-mode stage, and on the rising edge Q1 briefly cuts off (Vgs dips to about 0.6V). For that interval TP5 is pulled up passively by R21 into the drain node. The drain node's effective capacitance is about 105pF (IRF730 Coss and Crss, plus R31 and Cload), so the time constant is about 1us.

A grounded housing adds roughly Ccase/3 to that node. It also adds a slow distributed-RC tail just below the +100V knee where Q1 re-engages. The 10/90% rise metric is sensitive to that knee, which is why the rise jumps from 60pF to 100pF.

A floating housing only moves charge between internal nodes, so it adds roughly Ccase/12, about 4x less.

## Conclusion

- Oscillation or limit cycles: none, in all 61 cases.
  - Zero-input pp at TP5 is at most 35uV, and gate pp is 31uV.
  - Every flat window is under 1mV. The largest, 0.81mV, is the tail of the -140V to +140V step.
  - Every edge settles, and no edge rings in the MHz range.
  - Loop phase margin is at least 63° everywhere.
  - The phase reaches -180° below 300MHz at only 18 of 183 operating points, and at those the gain margin is at least 28dB.
  - R21's parasitics are therefore not a stability risk to this loop. They load the drain, which reaches the loop only through Cgd.
- Range that stays within 10% of ideal on rise and fall, with no oscillation:
  - Grounded housing, Cload 10pF: Ccase up to 15pF (the 10% crossing is about 19pF, and the fall edge is the limiting one) with L up to 0.5mH.
  - Grounded housing, Cload 30pF: Ccase up to 10pF (the rise crosses 10% at about 12pF).
  - Floating housing: Ccase up to about 60pF passes (simulated at 50uH; L up to 50uH changes the edges by under 1%). 100pF fails at L = 0 (+13% fall) and is marginal at 200uH.
  - Cpar up to 3pF is fine (at most 4.7%).
- Maximum inductance: about 0.5mH. Series inductance speeds the edges up and adds overshoot. It fails at 1mH (-18% fall, 9.4% overshoot), and from 2mH it rings at about 0.2MHz. All of the 0 to 200uH target range is harmless (at most 3.3%, overshoot +1.1 points).
- Maximum case capacitance with the housing grounded: about 15pF (10pF if Cload can be 30pF).
- Is grounding the housing acceptable? Yes for stability and power. For speed, only if the part's element-to-housing capacitance is at most about 15pF.
  - At 30pF, a grounded housing costs 9 to 16% in edge time and 5.5V of 100kHz swing.
  - At 100pF it costs about 55% and 21V, but it is still stable.
  - Floating the housing cuts that penalty about 4x, but leaves a metal housing at an undefined potential next to ±150V circuitry, so it is not recommended.
  - Tying the housing to +HV instead of GND should behave the same as grounding, because C26 makes +HV an AC ground. This is by inspection; it was not simulated.
  - Next step: measure the real part's lead-to-housing capacitance, with both leads shorted, on an LCR meter at 100kHz to 1MHz. Wirewound inductance for a 10kohm part (tens to hundreds of uH) is not a concern.

See V2-SUMMARY.md section 2.8 for how this budget applies to the final board layout.

## Runs

- 61 cases x 2 netlists = 122 runs, all ok, with 0 timeouts and 0 measurement failures in the final data (no .meas FAIL, no numpy metric errors).
  - The cases are the 25-point grid, 5 floating, 9 Cpar and 6 Cload 30pF spot checks, and 16 extension cases.
  - There is also 1 validation run (ok).
  - LTSpice time: about 713s for the transients and 382s for the loop runs.
- In the parallel sweep, the 4 L=1uH, Ccase>0 transients timed out. Run one at a time, the same netlists finish in about 5s. Each LTSpice process is multithreaded, so running several at once, or next to other LTSpice jobs, can push cases past the 240s timeout. Rerun any case that times out, one at a time.

## Caveats

- The IRF730 is an approximate VDMOS model from the datasheet, so absolute edge times depend on its Coss and Crss. The changes against ideal are the robust result.
- The U7 input is ideal. On the board it comes from Q3 into R17 2.7k.
- The R21 ladder is a 5-section model of a distributed winding.
- No temperature effects or R21 tolerance are modeled.
